RTX 5090 vs RTX 5070: A GPU Buying Guide for People Who Hate Wasting Money

The RTX 5090 costs $1,999 with 32GB of GDDR7; the RTX 5070 costs $549 with 12GB. Both are Blackwell, both play the same games. The right choice is almost entirely about your monitor.

NVIDIA's Blackwell generation spans an enormous range: the GeForce RTX 5090 launches at $1,999 with 32GB of GDDR7 memory, 21,760 CUDA cores and a 575W power draw, while the RTX 5070 launches at $549 with 12GB of GDDR7, 6,144 CUDA cores and a 250W draw. Same architecture family, nearly 4x the price.

Both cards support the same feature set — DLSS, current ray tracing, DisplayPort 2.1 and HDMI 2.1 outputs. The difference is raw throughput, and whether your monitor can actually show it.

Start with your monitor, not the card

This is the entire buying guide in one rule. At 1080p, even the RTX 5070 is more than most games need, and the 5090's extra power is invisible — your monitor physically cannot display the difference. At 1440p high-refresh, the 5070 is the sweet spot and the 5080 ($999) is the comfortable ceiling.

The RTX 5090 only makes sense at 4K high-refresh, for heavy ray tracing at 4K, or for GPU compute work where 32GB of VRAM is a working requirement rather than a luxury. If none of those describe you, the 5090 is not a better purchase — it is a more expensive one.

  • 1080p gaming: RTX 5060-class is enough; 5070 is comfortable headroom
  • 1440p high-refresh: RTX 5070 ($549) is the value pick, RTX 5080 ($999) the ceiling
  • 4K high-refresh or heavy ray tracing: RTX 5090 ($1,999) territory
  • GPU compute / AI work: buy VRAM first — the 5090's 32GB GDDR7 is the real product

Don't forget the rest of the system cost

A 575W card is not a $1,999 purchase — it is a $1,999 card plus a high-wattage quality PSU, a case with real airflow and the electricity to feed it. The 5070's 250W draw slots into far more existing builds without upgrades.

AMD's Radeon RX 9070 XT at $599 deserves a mention here as the 5070's direct rival: competitive rasterization performance at a similar price, which keeps NVIDIA honest in the mid-range. Our head-to-head comparison page covers that matchup directly.

The upgrade question: 40-series owners

If you own an RTX 4070-class card or better and game at 1440p, there is no urgency — you already own the sweet spot. RTX 4090 owners should note the 5090 is a real step up (more cores, more VRAM, more power), but only 4K high-refresh gamers will feel it.

The buyers who gain most from Blackwell are those on RTX 3060/3070-era cards or older: the generational jump in ray tracing and upscaling quality is large, and even a 5070 will feel like a new computer.

Bottom line

Match the card to the monitor: 5070 for 1440p, 5080 for demanding 1440p/entry 4K, 5090 for 4K high-refresh or compute. Budget for the PSU and cooling the card actually needs, and check our comparison pages for the 5070 vs RX 9070 XT and 5090 vs 4090 head-to-heads before you spend.

Launch prices are a starting point, not a promise — street prices move with supply, bundles and sales. Verify the current retailer price before buying.
